# Sources & References

## Primary Sources

### Icelandic Sagas
- *Grettis Saga Ásmundarsonar* (13th century). Various translations.
  - Primary source for Glámr the draugr, most famous draugr account
- *Eyrbyggja Saga* (13th century). Various translations.
  - Multiple draugr accounts including Thorolf Twist-Foot
- *Laxdæla Saga* (13th century). Various translations.
  - Account of Hrapp the draugr
- *Njáls Saga* (13th century). Various translations.
  - References to draugr and undead
- *Gísla Saga Súrssonar* (13th century). Various translations.

### Collections
- *The Sagas of Icelanders: A Selection*. (2000). Penguin Classics.
  - Accessible modern translations of key sagas
- Byock, J. (translator). (2009). *Grettir's Saga*. Oxford World's Classics.
- Pálsson, H. & Edwards, P. (translators). (1989). *Eyrbyggja Saga*. Penguin.

## Academic Books

### Norse Mythology & Folklore
- Lecouteux, C. (2009). *The Return of the Dead: Ghosts, Ancestors, and the Transparent Veil of the Pagan Mind*. Inner Traditions. ISBN: 978-1594773181
  - Comprehensive study of Norse undead including draugr
- Lecouteux, C. (2013). *The Secret History of Vampires: Their Multiple Forms and Hidden Purposes*. Inner Traditions.
  - Includes draugr in vampire context
- Davidson, H.R.E. (1993). *The Lost Beliefs of Northern Europe*. Routledge.
  - Context for Norse beliefs about death

### Death & Burial Practices
