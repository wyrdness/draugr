## Overview

The Draugr is a corporeal undead creature from Norse mythology and Icelandic sagas, distinguished from ghosts by its physical, animated body. Characterized by superhuman strength, the ability to increase dramatically in size, and a blackish or dark appearance, draugr guard their burial mounds and treasure hoards with jealous, murderous ferocity.

These are not mindless zombies—draugr retain their personality and intelligence from life, often displaying the same greed, violence, and malevolence that characterized them as living men. They possess various supernatural powers including shape-shifting, immunity to normal weapons, the ability to drive people mad, and occasionally prophecy. Draugr emit a distinctive smell of decay and death that announces their presence.

They can be defeated through beheading, burning the body, or heroic combat by warriors of great strength and courage. The sagas are filled with accounts of heroes battling these formidable undead guardians.

## Etymology & Names

### Primary Name
- **Draugr** — From Old Norse 'draugr', possibly related to 'ghost' or from 'drjúgr' meaning 'lasting'. The name evokes the undead, revenants, and those who walk after death.

## Notable Accounts

### Glámr the Draugr ⭐

> **Source**: Grettis Saga (13th century)
> **Period**: Earlier events, recorded later

Glámr was a shepherd of unusual strength and bad temper who died under mysterious circumstances on Christmas Eve. He returned as a powerful draugr, terrorizing the region with superhuman strength and the ability to break men's courage with his gaze.

The hero Grettir Ásmundarson fought Glámr in an epic nighttime battle inside a farmhouse. Though Grettir prevailed, Glámr cursed him with his dying words, causing Grettir to fear the dark for the rest of his life.

**Significance**: Most famous draugr account in saga literature, illustrating both the physical danger and psychological horror of encountering the undead.

---

### Hrapp of Hrappsey

> **Source**: Laxdæla Saga

Hrapp was an evil, violent man who promised to cause trouble after death. True to his word, he became a draugr haunting his farm, killing livestock and people. His hauntings only ceased after his body was properly dealt with.

**Significance**: Example of personality continuing after death—evil in life, evil in death.

---

### Thorolf Twist-Foot

> **Source**: Eyrbyggja Saga

Thorolf was buried improperly and became a draugr. His haunting caused death and misfortune until his body was exhumed, burned, and the ashes scattered.

**Significance**: Illustrates the critical importance of proper burial rites in preventing draugr creation.

## Cultural Significance

### Norse/Viking Cultural Context

> ⚠️ **Cultural Context Note**: Part of historical Norse cultural heritage, actively studied and preserved in modern Scandinavia. Recognize as important historical belief system.

**Traditional Beliefs**:
- Proper burial rites prevent draugr creation
- Stone doors on burial mounds provide protection
- Beheading and burning of dangerous dead
- Burial with possessions to satisfy the dead
- Christian burial to prevent rising

**Significance**: Draugr represent the danger of greed, improper burial, and evil nature continuing beyond death. They enforce the necessity of proper treatment of the dead.

### Relevant Cultures

Norse, Icelandic, Norwegian, Swedish, Danish

Central to Norse understanding of death and proper burial. Reflects Viking Age beliefs about the power of the dead and the importance of proper rites.
